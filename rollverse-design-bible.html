01

The Name

It has to work when the game is just skateboards — and still work the day it has scooters and electric boards. So the name shouldn't say "skate." It should say roll.

Recommended

Rollverse

  • A universe of parks. The "-verse" is literally the connected world of districts you travel between.
  • Rolls with anything. Skateboard, scooter, e-board, e-scooter — they all roll. No rename needed, ever.
  • Kid-sayable, brandable, .com-ish energy. One punchy word, easy to chant.
  • Icon-friendly. A wheel = the universal symbol for the whole game (see next section).

Strong alternates

FreewheelFree-play + wheels; calm, cool, universal. Slightly less "world."
Grind CitySkate-flavored + city (matches the roads/parks world). Leans skate-only.
RollparkSays "parks you roll in" plainly. A touch generic.
ShredPure attitude, one syllable. Very skate; harder to own/search.
SidewalkWarm, wholesome, city-street feel. Understated for a kid.
Design note

Whatever we pick, keep a sub-title slot ("Rollverse: Street Season") so seasons/updates get names without touching the brand. Let Mateo do the final gut-check out loud — the right name is the one he wants to say to his friends.

02

The Icon

One idea does all the work: a wheel. It reads instantly, it's true to every rideable in the game (skate, scooter, electric), and it still looks like something at 40 px on a home screen. Two takes below — same brand, same dusk-park palette as the game.

Orbit Wheel

The wheel + an orbit ring = the "-verse" of connected parks. More story, best at larger sizes.

Bold Wheel

Higher contrast, one confident swoosh. Stays legible when tiny — the safer home-screen pick.

Why a wheel

A board or a skater would lock the icon to skateboarding. A bearing-style wheel is the one part every rideable shares — so the brand mark survives the jump to scooters and electric with zero redraw. The cyan bearing balls double as a tiny "spark" that hints at the electric future.

03

Design Pillars

Five rules that keep every future decision pointed the same way.

1 · Free play first

Roam, mess around, no timer. Goals are layered on top of the sandbox, never in place of it.

2 · Real, made friendly

Real tricks, real gear, real physics feel — with big assists so a kid lands things and grins.

3 · One "rideable" system

Skateboard, scooter, e-board are configs of one thing. New rides = data, not rewrites.

4 · The world is alive

Roads, people, traffic. Precision matters — dodging a crowd is a skill, smashing it has consequences.

5 · Change → feel it now

Tune a slider or swap a wheel and feel the difference instantly. Playing doubles as designing.

The World: Connected Parks

The sweet spot for a kid is a hybrid of Tony Hawk (distinct themed parks) and OlliOlli (an overworld you travel) — themed districts joined by short roads you actually skate, not a menu. Unlocking a new district feels like an adventure, not a click.

Each district = one real park "biome" (so each feels different to skate)

From research

Almost every real obstacle decomposes into just two engine verbs: carve/pump on curves and grind/slide/manual on edges. Nail those two and a ~20-piece obstacle palette (quarter, half, mini, bowl, spine, ledge, manual pad, funbox, pyramid, rail, flat bar, stairs, hubba, bank, euro gap, kicker, pump track, snake run, mega ramp) covers the whole vocabulary — and doubles as a future create-a-park toolset.

05

People, Roads & the Heat System

Mateo's rule: don't smash into people. This is what makes the living world matter — it turns "jump over people" from a gimmick into a real skill, with real (funny) consequences.

✔ Good contact = style

  • Ollie over a pedestrian → "HOP! +style".
  • Near-miss weave through a crowd → "Close call!" bonus.
  • Clean lines through busy areas build your combo instead of breaking it.

✘ Bad contact = trouble

  • Smash a person → lose points, break combo, gain Heat.
  • Heat cools down over time if you skate clean.
  • Fill the Heat meter → a Security Guard / Park Ranger gives chase.
  • Get caught → short, cartoon "Skate Jail" timeout, then released.

The Heat meter (kid-friendly, slapstick — not GTA)

Clean skating→ Bump someone (+Heat)→ Meter fills→ Guard chase→ Caught = brief timeout→ Released, Heat reset
Make it teach, not just punish

Add a make-amends path: after a bump, a quick "say sorry" tap or landing a cool trick charms the crowd and lowers Heat. So the lesson is "be careful around people — and if you mess up, make it right," not just "avoid humans." Roads add the same tension with traffic: skating a busy street is a dodge mini-game; nailing it clean = big style, botching it = Heat.

The Rideable System the adaptability engine

This is the most important decision in the whole project. Never hardcode "skateboard." Everything you ride is one data-driven Rideable = shared base + a few swappable modules. Skateboards, scooters, e-boards and e-scooters become configs, so the expansion Mateo wants is adding data, not rebuilding the game.

What every rideable shares (one core)

Wheels size · hardness · grip Deck width · length · weight · flex Momentum speed · inertia Lean/carve steering Terrain grip · bumps · hills · air Landing clean / sketchy / bail Trick score air × rotation × clean × combo

What differs (swappable modules — just four things really matter)

ModuleSkateboardStunt ScooterE-BoardE-Scooter
Speed upFoot pushFoot pushThrottleThrottle
Slow downFoot / slideRear flex brakeRegen (remote)Disc / drum / regen
HandsFreeHold barsFree (remote)Hold bars
Trick verbFlips / shove-its (free-space)Whips / barspins (pivot on bars)Carve / slide linesJumps / bar tricks
Battery?——✔ manage Wh✔ manage Wh
The whole abstraction in one line

Everything shares wheels, a deck, momentum, terrain, and a "keep control + score the air" loop. The only things that truly differ are how you speed up (push vs. throttle), how you slow down (foot/flex vs. regen/disc), whether you're holding bars (which flips the whole trick vocabulary from flips to whips), and whether there's a battery to manage. Model those four as modules and one system covers all four sports — plus future ones (onewheel, BMX, e-unicycle) drop in for free.

Bonus: the "garage" is a teaching tool

Because parts are real and data-driven, a garage screen lets Mateo tune wheel hardness, truck tightness, board width — and feel the change on the next run. That's the same live-iteration idea as the current prototype's Studio, extended to real gear. Playing the game teaches real skateboarding.

07

Real Tricks & the Skill Tree

The real trick space isn't a list — it's a small list multiplied by stance and rotation. That's what gives a skate game surprising depth from a handful of core moves. Everything below is real (verified), and ordered the way people actually learn.

Flip tricks real

Board flips/spins off the feet in free space.

T1Ollie→ T2Shove-it→ T2Kickflip→ T3Heelflip→ T3Varial flip→ T4360 flip (tre)→ T5Hardflip · Laser

Grinds real (metal trucks contact)

T150-50→ T25-0 · Nosegrind→ T3Crooked (K-grind)→ T4Smith · Feeble→ T5Overcrook · spin-outs

Slides real (the deck contacts)

T1Boardslide→ T2Noseslide→ T3Tailslide→ T3Lipslide→ T5Bluntslide · Darkslide

Air & grabs real (ramps/bowls)

T1Drop-in · Pump→ T2Rock to fakie→ T3Indy · Mute · Melon→ T4Stalefish · Method→ T5Christ air · McTwist

The multiplier that creates depth

  • Stance ×4: every flip exists as regular / fakie / switch / nollie (kickflip → nollie kickflip…).
  • Rotation: frontside or backside, at 180 / 360 / 540.
  • Combine: flip into a grind or slide ("kickflip backside tailslide"), spin out of it.
  • Combo grammar: [stance] [rotation] [flip] [obstacle trick] [exit] — e.g. "switch frontside 360 kickflip".
Scoring, from the real games

Base value × combo multiplier. Manuals and grinds are the glue that links tricks into one long line; a revert→manual connects ramp tricks together (the trick that made THPS combos endless). Repeating the same trick lowers its value (forces variety); switch/nollie scores more; a bail wipes the uncommitted combo. For young players: generous landing windows + auto-catch assists = they land things and keep grinning.

Real Gear (that actually changes how you ride)

Gear shouldn't be just skins. Real parts have real tradeoffs — so choosing a setup becomes a light strategy layer, and the game quietly teaches how skateboards work.

Deck width = the feel dial real

WidthFlipsStableBest for
7.5–7.9"FastestLowTech street, small feet
8.0–8.25"FastMediumAll-around (most popular)
8.5–9.0"SlowerHighRamps, bowls, big air

Wheels: grip ↔ speed real

  • Diameter: 50–54mm = light, quick, street; 60mm+ = fast, rolls over rough ground, cruising.
  • Durometer (hardness): 78a–87a soft = grippy & smooth but slow; 99a–101a hard = fast & slides easy but chattery.
  • In game: soft wheels help The Hills & rough roads; hard wheels win in the park.

Trucks: turn ↔ stability real

  • Match width to the deck (axle within ~¼" of deck width).
  • Tighter kingpin / harder bushings = stable at speed; looser / softer = carvy and quick to turn.
  • Low trucks suit flips + small wheels; high trucks clear big cruiser wheels.

Bearings: the honest truth real

  • All skate bearings are the 608 size (8×22×7mm).
  • ABEC ratings are mostly marketing for skating — they measure factory precision, not durability or real-world speed. Bones skips ABEC entirely with its own "Skate Rated" standard.
  • In game: model bearings as "how long you keep rolling / how smooth," and let dirt & water matter more than the ABEC number — a fun, true detail.

CruiserShaped deck + kicktail, soft wheelsGetting around, comfortNot for tech tricks

09

Scooters & Electric (the expansion)

Because of the Rideable System, these slot in as new unlockable categories with their own districts and trick trees — no re-architecture. Here's what makes each one feel distinct.

Stunt scooter real

You hold the bars, so tricks are whips & spins around an anchor, not free-space flips.

T1Bunny hop→ T2Bar spin→ T3Tailwhip→ T4Bri flip→ T5Flair · Buttercup

Parts: bars, clamp, deck, fork, 110mm PU wheels, flex brake. Tuning: compression (SCS strongest, IHC/HIC lightest).

Electric skateboard real

No pushing. Throttle + regen braking. The skill is speed, carving & hills, not flips.

  • Hub vs belt motors; battery in Wh sets range, voltage sets top speed.
  • Ride modes: Eco → Sport → Pro (throttle & brake strength).
  • Real range: ~18–40 mph, ~7–40 miles. New failure states: battery %, speed wobble.

Home district: The Hills — carving lines & hill bombs.

Electric scooter real

Throttle + disc/regen brakes, suspension, folding. More commute/traffic & speed than tricks.

  • Single vs dual motor; pneumatic (grip) vs solid (no flats) tyres.
  • Commuter ~15–20 mph → performance 40–50+ mph.
  • Slots into the city roads perfectly — a different way to travel between parks.
Shared skeleton, different verbs

All four score the same way — air time × rotation × clean landing × combo. A single flag, ControlAnchor = hands-free / handlebars, reshapes the entire trick set and animation rig. That one switch is what lets skateboards and scooters coexist without doubling the code.

Progression, Goals & Controls

The kid-proven loop real

THPS's genius: many small goals stacked on one short, restartable run in a sandbox. A wipeout costs seconds, not minutes.

  • Per-park goals: high score, S-K-A-T-E letters, a signature gap, "interact with X", hidden collectible.
  • Complete N goals → unlock the next district (unlock = travel).
  • Beginner grabs one letter; expert does all six goals in one line — same park, every skill level.

Rewards & identity

  • Style points → currency → boards, wheels, scooters, outfits, new parks.
  • Skill tree unlocks trick families in the real learning order (ollie → shove → kickflip → grinds → spins → switch).
  • Create-a-skater + eventually create-a-park (reuses the obstacle palette) = ownership & endless replay.

Controls — instant to play, deep to master

TierSchemeFor
Simple (v1 today)Joystick move · tap = jump · tap-in-air = auto-trickAny kid, first 5 minutes
IntermediateDirectional trick buttons (THPS-style) + hold for grinds/manualsGetting hooked
Advanced (optional)Flick gestures / combo timing, reverts, manual linkingSkill ceiling
Recommendation

Lead with a THPS-style button scheme (predictable, forgiving, instantly fun) with big assists and generous landing windows — that's the difference between a kid iterating happily and rage-quitting. Offer a simplified flick control as an "advanced" toggle later. The same input abstraction serves every rideable; bar-vehicles just remap "flip" inputs to "whip/spin".

The Road to the Games career ladder

This is the long-term dream that gives all the free-play a destination: start as an unknown skating the plaza, and climb a ladder of real-style competitions all the way to an Olympic-style "Rollverse Games." The best part — the real Olympic skateboarding formats map exactly onto the world we're already building.

Real grounding

Skateboarding is a real Olympic sport (debuted Tokyo 2020, again Paris 2024) with two events: Street (a course of stairs, rails, ledges and gaps) and Park (a flowing bowl/pool course). Riders get judged runs plus best-tricks, scored 0–100 on difficulty, variety, execution, flow and consistency. So our Marble Blocks = the Street event and Beachside Bowl = the Park event — the districts are the arenas.

The ladder — earn your way up (unlock = an invitation)

TierEventHow you get inReward
1Street Sessions (free-play jams)Just skate — beat a target score in a districtFirst Rep points; local rep
2Local Jam (best-trick)Reach a Rep thresholdCash, a sponsor sticker, a new deck
3Regional Open (Street or Park)Podium at a Local JamRanking points, gear tier unlock
4Pro Tour Stop (qualifiers → finals)Top ranking + required trick-tree unlocksPro status, signature setup
5National Team spotWin a Pro Tour stopTeam kit; invite to the Games
★The Rollverse Games (Olympic Park)Qualify through the tourThe gold — and a golden board/scooter

How a competition plays (uses systems we already have)

Format

  • Runs: a timed line through the course — your best run counts (drop the worst), just like the real thing.
  • Best trick: a jam where only your single biggest trick scores.
  • Qualifiers → Finals: place high enough to advance.

Judging (0–100) — teach what makes skating good

  • Difficulty — harder tricks score more (our skill-tree tiers).
  • Variety — repeating a trick is penalised (already in the combo engine).
  • Execution & flow — clean landings, no bails, keep momentum.
  • Consistency — land the whole run, not one lucky trick.
Why it's the perfect capstone

It reuses everything: the districts become arenas, the skill tree becomes entry requirements, the scoring engine becomes the judges, and gear choice becomes strategy. It answers "what am I working toward?" with a dream a kid understands — the Olympics — and it generalises across rideables: a Scooter circuit (park & street scooter comps are real) and, later, electric race/downhill events all hang off the same ladder. One system, a lifetime of goals.

Keep it kind

Losing a heat should feel like "so close — try again," never a dead end. Show what would've moved you up ("one more clean trick"), keep entry fees tiny, and always leave the free-play world open between events. The ladder is a pull, not a gate.
